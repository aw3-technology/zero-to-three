# 7-Day Idea Exploration Sprint Template

> **Standalone downloadable resource** from *Zero to Three*, Chapter 08: Idea Development.
> Also available interactively at **zeroto3.book/sprint**

---

## Overview

This sprint compresses the messy early stages of idea validation into a structured seven-day process. By the end, you will have gathered sources, conducted interviews, built a prototype artifact, and scored your idea against a weighted rubric. The goal is not to prove your idea is great — it's to learn fast whether it's worth pursuing.

**What you need to start:** a rough idea or problem area, access to potential users, and 4–6 focused hours per day for seven days.

---

## Day 1–2: Source Gathering

- [ ] Read 10 recent academic papers in your domain
- [ ] Identify 5 adjacent industries with similar problems
- [ ] Review patent filings and prior art for inspiration
- [ ] Collect weak signals from crypto Twitter, research blogs, and Discord
- [ ] List 3 assumptions your idea depends on being true
- [ ] Identify who would lose if your idea succeeds (competitive displacement map)

**Output:** A research brief (1–2 pages) summarizing what you found and what surprised you.

---

## Day 3–4: Interview and Validation

- [ ] Interview 8 potential users (focus on pain, not solutions)
- [ ] Talk to 3 technical experts in the space
- [ ] Reach out to 2 investors who've funded similar companies
- [ ] Document common themes and surprising insights
- [ ] Note exact quotes that reveal emotional intensity around the problem
- [ ] Identify the "hair on fire" segment — who needs this most urgently?

**Output:** An interview synthesis with top 5 themes and 3 direct quotes.

---

## Day 5–6: Prototype and Artifacts

- [ ] Build a 48-hour proof-of-concept or wireframe
- [ ] Create a technical design doc outlining approach
- [ ] Draft value proposition and positioning narrative
- [ ] Map competitive landscape and differentiation
- [ ] Write a one-paragraph "press release from the future" describing success
- [ ] Identify the single hardest technical risk and how you'd mitigate it

**Output:** A clickable prototype or technical sketch, plus a one-page positioning doc.

---

## Day 7: Evaluation and Decision

- [ ] Score idea using the rubric below
- [ ] Get feedback from 3 trusted advisors
- [ ] Write investment thesis for yourself
- [ ] Decide: pursue, pivot, or pause
- [ ] If pursuing: write down the three things you'll do in the next 7 days

**Output:** A completed scorecard and a go/no-go decision with reasoning.

---

## Idea Scoring Rubric

Rate each dimension 1–10, multiply by weight:

| **Dimension** | **Weight** | **Score (1–10)** | **Weighted Score** |
|---------------|------------|------------------|--------------------|
| **Contrarian Truth** — Non-obvious insight others miss | 25% | ___ | ___ |
| **Technical Feasibility** — Can you build it with current tech? | 20% | ___ | ___ |
| **Market Pull** — Do people desperately want this? | 20% | ___ | ___ |
| **Defensible Moat** — Is it hard to replicate? | 15% | ___ | ___ |
| **Timing** — Why now and not two years ago? | 10% | ___ | ___ |
| **Founder-Market Fit** — Are you uniquely suited? | 10% | ___ | ___ |

**Total Weighted Score:** ___/100

### Scoring Guidelines

- **80+**: Strong candidate — proceed to build
- **60–79**: Has potential — needs refinement on weak dimensions
- **40–59**: Significant concerns — major pivot needed
- **Below 40**: Move on to next idea

---

## Tips for Running the Sprint

1. **Time-box ruthlessly.** The point is speed, not perfection. A messy prototype on Day 6 beats a polished slide deck on Day 14.
2. **Interview for pain, not validation.** Ask "what's the hardest part of X?" not "would you use a product that does Y?"
3. **Score honestly.** The rubric only helps if you resist inflating your own scores. Have an advisor score independently and compare.
4. **Document everything.** Your Day 1 notes will be valuable six months from now, regardless of whether you pursue this idea.
5. **Decide on Day 7.** The sprint's greatest value is forcing a decision. Ambiguity is the enemy of progress.
